Chapter 1: Every page here can be read two ways

Welcome. This short document is not a manual. It is a piece of reading material, exactly like anything you will upload later, and it teaches EasyRead by letting you use the app on it. Read it the way you would read anything else. When it asks you to try something, try it, then come back to the text.

The original and the simplified

Every document you bring into EasyRead exists twice. There is the original, exactly as its author wrote it, and there is a simplified version written in plainer language. The simplified version keeps every technical term, every name, and every number exact. What changes is the sentence around them: shorter, cleaner, easier to hold in your head.

You are probably reading the simplified version of this page right now. The toggle at the top of the screen switches between the two views, and on a computer the letter V on your keyboard flips them instantly. On a large screen you can also put them side by side.

Try it now. Switch to the Original view, look at this same page, then switch back. Nothing is lost between the two. That is the point.

Two levels of plain

Sometimes the simplified version is still too dense. There is a second level called Easiest that goes further, using shorter sentences and more everyday words, while still keeping every term you will be examined on. You will find the level switch next to the view toggle. Standard is right for most reading. Easiest is there for the pages that fight back.

Make the page comfortable

Reading is physical. The comfort control in the toolbar changes the text size, the spacing between lines, the font, and the color of the page itself, including a warm sepia and a dark mode. Small changes here make long reading sessions much easier on your eyes. Set it once and every document remembers.

Try it now. Open the comfort control and make the text one step bigger. If it reads better, keep it.

Chapter 2: What to do when a line will not sink in

Everyone hits sentences that refuse to go in. In a paper book you reread the same line five times and feel worse each time. Here, the sentence itself is something you can act on.

Select it

Select any sentence on this page with your mouse or finger, the way you would to copy it. A small menu appears with everything you can do to that exact passage.

Explain rewrites the idea in context and tells you why it matters here. Simplify says the same thing in plainer words. Define is for a single term you have never met. Each answer arrives in the chat at the side, tied to the passage you chose, so you can ask a follow-up question in your own words.

Try it now. Select the sentence "Reading is physical" back in chapter one, or any line on this page, and tap Explain.

Keep it

The same menu can save the passage instead of explaining it. Highlight paints it and keeps the exact words in your notes, so you can find your way back to this page later. Ask opens the chat with the passage already quoted, waiting for whatever question you actually have. Note lets you write your own thought against the passage. Everything you keep lives in the Notes panel, and each saved item can take you back to the page it came from.

Try it now. Select a line you like and tap Highlight. Then find it painted on the page, and again in the Notes panel under More.

Ask the document anything

The chat knows this whole document, not just the passage you selected. Ask it what a chapter was about, where something was mentioned, or how two ideas connect, and it answers from these pages with page references you can check. If you would rather listen than read, the Listen button reads any page aloud. If you would rather talk, the microphone opens a voice conversation about what you are reading.

Chapter 3: How to make what you read stay

Reading a page and remembering a page are different skills. Most people finish a chapter, feel like they understood it, and discover a week later that almost nothing stayed. The feeling of understanding is not the same as memory. This last chapter is about the tools that close that gap.

Guided reading

Guided reading is a mode you switch on from the toolbar. It leaves you alone while you read and appears only at the edges of chapters. Before a chapter starts, it shows you the shape of what is coming and lets you pose your own questions. When a chapter ends, it asks you to say the chapter back from memory, in your own words, with the page out of sight. Then it checks what you said against the chapter itself and shows you exactly which ideas came back and which did not.

That moment of saying it back is the single most powerful thing in this app. It feels harder than rereading. That difficulty is the memory being made.

Try it now. Turn on Guided reading from the toolbar. When you reach the end of this chapter, it will catch you, and you can try saying this document back.

Your understanding, measured honestly

Every check you do is remembered. The Your understanding screen shows how each chapter of a document is going: which ones are solid, which are settling, and which need another pass. It never compares you to anyone else. It compares you to your own last attempt, tells you the exact ideas that keep slipping, and offers one next step, like rereading a few pages with those ideas in hand, or handing the chapter to the tutor.

A tutor who has read what you read

Teach me, in the toolbar, starts a voice lesson on whatever you are reading with a tutor who has read the document and knows what you have been getting stuck on. It draws diagrams, asks you questions, and adjusts to your answers. Reading alone is fine most days. On the days it is not enough, this is the other door.

That is the whole app. Upload something you have been avoiding, and read it the way it should have been written in the first place.
